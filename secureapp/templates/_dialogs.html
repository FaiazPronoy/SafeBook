What happens to what you post

Every post is locked before it is saved, with a key made just for that post. Then copies of that key are handed to the people allowed to read it, and to nobody else.

Your own copy is locked with your password. While you are signed in SafeBook can use it to show you your things; the moment you sign out, it is gone. What is left on the server is scrambled text that nobody there can turn back into words.

That is also why nobody here can reset your password for you. A new password would not open the old lock. Your recovery code is the second key, and the only other way in.

The technical version

Each account has a random 256 bit data key, sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from your password with scrypt, and sealed again under a key from your recovery code. Every post has its own AES-256 content key; your copy of it is sealed under your data key.

Only me, Friends and Public

Only me. The only copy of the post's key is yours, locked with your password. Nobody else holds a key to it, SafeBook included.

Friends. Each friend gets a copy of the key, locked with their own public key, so only their password can open it. The server stores every copy and can open none of them. Unfriend someone and their copies are deleted.

Public. SafeBook keeps a copy under its own server key, because a public post has to be shown to people who hold no key at all.

Changing the audience hands out or takes back copies of the key. The post itself is never re-encrypted, however long it is.

The technical version

A friend's copy is the 32 byte content key wrapped with their RSA-2048 public key using OAEP with SHA-256. A new friend's copies are made the next time the author is signed in, because only the author can unwrap their own content keys. Comments are encrypted with the post's content key, so the same people can read them.

A key already handed out cannot be un-read: someone who opened your post while you were friends may have kept what they saw. Unfriending protects what you post afterwards.

How a chat message is sealed

Every account has a pair of matched locks. One half is public, so anyone can lock something for you. The other half is yours, and it is the only thing that opens what they locked.

Each message gets a fresh key. That key is locked twice: once with the other person's public half, once with yours, so both of you can scroll back through the chat. Nobody else holds a copy.

The technical version

AES-256-GCM with a fresh session key per message, wrapped with RSA-OAEP for the recipient and the sender. Your RSA private key is itself encrypted under your data key, so opening a message needs your password. Each row also carries an HMAC over both ids and the ciphertext.

What a safety number is for

Every account has a short code of its own, worked out from that account's public lock. It changes if the lock ever changes.

Read yours out to a friend in person or on a call, and check it against what SafeBook shows them on your profile. If the two match, the keys they hand you really reach you.

If they ever stop matching, stop and ask why. This is the one check software cannot do for you, because the whole point is that it does not go through SafeBook.

The technical version

The safety number is a SHA-256 digest of the account's public key, shown in groups of four. Comparing it out of band is what catches a server handing you its own public key in place of your friend's.

Your password is also your key

Most sites use your password to check that you are you. Here it does that and one more thing: it unlocks the key to your posts and chats.

So changing it is quick and safe. Your posts are not touched at all. Only the small lock that holds your key is rebuilt around the new password.

It also means a weak password is a weak lock, not just a weak login. A long one you can remember beats a short complicated one.

The technical version

An Argon2id hash verifies the password. Separately, scrypt derives a 32 byte wrapping key from the same password, and that key seals your data key. Changing your password rewrites one sealed envelope.

Why the recovery code matters

Because your password is what unlocks your things, forgetting it would normally mean losing them. There is nobody here who can open your account for you.

The recovery code is a second lock around the same key. Keep it somewhere real, away from the device you sign in on. It is shown once, and SafeBook keeps no copy it can read.

Anyone holding it can get into your account, so treat it like a spare front door key.

Signing in

A wrong password takes the same time whether or not the account exists, and says the same thing either way. Otherwise this page would tell strangers which names are real.

Five wrong attempts in a row pause that name for fifteen minutes, so nobody can sit there working through a list of guesses.

After fifteen minutes without activity SafeBook signs you out by itself, because a signed-in tab is the one place your key is open.

Why something might not open

Every post, comment and message carries a small seal, taken over the locked text when it was saved. Before anything is shown to you, that seal is checked.

If even one character of the stored text has changed since, the seal will not match and SafeBook will not show it. Not a damaged version with a warning beside it: nothing, because a warning you can read past is not really a check.

The technical version

Each row carries an HMAC-SHA256 over a length prefixed encoding of its ciphertext and the ids it belongs to, keyed by a subkey derived from the row key with HKDF, and compared in constant time.
